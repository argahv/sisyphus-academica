The behaviour of the two tests under unequal variances is well documented. Ruxton~\citep{ruxton2006unequal} compared Student's test, Welch's test and the Mann-Whitney test across a range of variance ratios and concluded that Welch's test lost little power when variances were equal and avoided large error inflation when they were not. Delacre and colleagues~\citep{delacre2017why} reached the same recommendation for psychology and showed that tests of variance equality are too weak to decide between the two procedures.

Our study does not extend these results to new conditions. It isolates one configuration that appears often in practice, a small treatment group compared with a larger control group, and reports the size of the problem with intervals rather than point estimates.
